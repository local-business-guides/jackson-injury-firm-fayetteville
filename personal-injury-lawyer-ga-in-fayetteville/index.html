Get ready for a useful conversation

You don’t need a polished account. Before you call, jot down the basics in your own words: where the incident took place, what you remember, and what has changed since then. If there are details you aren’t sure about, mark them as uncertain rather than filling in the gaps.

You may also want to make a folder with any records you already have. Useful items can include:

  • Names and contact information for people who saw what happened
  • Photos of the location, damage or visible injuries
  • Medical visit information and instructions you were given
  • Emails, letters or forms from an insurance company
  • Notes about missed work or other practical effects

The folder is for your own preparation, not a test. Don’t delay a conversation just because you haven’t collected everything. The firm can explain what may matter and what could be gathered later.

Understand the working arrangement

Before deciding, ask how the firm evaluates a matter, what steps may follow, and what circumstances could lead to negotiation or litigation. Ask for a plain explanation of the contingency fee arrangement, including how it applies to your situation. Jackson Injury Firm offers free consultations, so you can raise these points before making a choice.

Clear expectations can make the working relationship easier. Find out how updates are shared and whether you can speak directly with the attorney. If you prefer to communicate in Spanish, the firm assists clients in English and Spanish. It’s fine to bring a family member to help you keep track of information, if that feels useful to you.

Keep your account accurate and organized

As you consider hiring a lawyer, keep copies of communications and make a short note after important calls. Record the date, the person you spoke with and any follow-up they mentioned. If new information comes to mind later, write it down with the date rather than changing your earlier notes.

Avoid signing papers you don’t understand or agreeing to terms simply because you feel rushed. Share questions about insurance forms or proposed resolutions with a lawyer before you respond.
