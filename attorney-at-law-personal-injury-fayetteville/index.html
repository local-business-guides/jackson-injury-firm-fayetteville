## A short checklist for your first conversation
A first meeting is a chance to describe what happened and learn whether the firm may be able to take the case. You don't need to arrive with every detail settled. If you can, bring or write down:

  • The date and general location of the incident.
  • A brief timeline of what happened, including anything you remember before and after.
  • Names and contact details for people who witnessed it, if you have them.
  • Photos, incident paperwork, or messages related to the event.
  • Medical visit information and a list of questions about how the injury has affected you.
  • Any letters, emails, or calls you've received from an insurance company.

Keep the original records where they are and share copies when requested. If you're missing something, say so. The attorney can tell you what may be useful to gather next.

## Mistakes that can make the process harder
Try not to guess when describing details you aren't sure about. It's fine to say you don't remember. Avoid throwing away paperwork or photos that could help explain what happened, and keep notes about new communications related to the incident.

Be careful about giving an insurer a detailed recorded account before you understand what it's asking and how the information may be used. You can ask for time to review a request and discuss it with a lawyer. Don't assume that a quick settlement offer covers every concern. Ask what the offer addresses and whether you have enough information to consider it.
