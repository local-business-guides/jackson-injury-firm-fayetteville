A short checklist before the first conversation

Gather what you already have, but don't delay a call just because some items are missing. Useful materials may include:

  • Photos or videos of the scene, vehicles, hazards or visible injuries.
  • Names and contact details for witnesses, if you have them.
  • Incident reports, claim numbers and letters or emails from an insurer.
  • Receipts or records related to treatment, repairs or other costs.
  • A simple timeline of appointments, calls and changes since the incident.

Keep originals when possible and make copies for your own records. If you communicate with an insurer, note the date, who you spoke with and what was discussed. This can give you a reliable reference later.

Mistakes that can make things harder

Try not to fill gaps in your account with assumptions, post detailed commentary about the incident online, or throw away documents that may relate to it. Be cautious about signing paperwork or agreeing to a settlement before you understand what it says and how it may affect your options. If you're being asked to respond quickly, you can ask for time to review the request and get legal guidance.

Don't assume that an unanswered question means your situation has no legal options. At the same time, an attorney can't promise a particular result before reviewing the facts. Bring up concerns plainly, including anything that might seem unfavorable. A complete picture is more useful than a carefully edited one.
