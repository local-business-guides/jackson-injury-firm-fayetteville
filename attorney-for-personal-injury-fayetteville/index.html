## Questions worth asking before you hire
Use the consultation to learn about the working relationship, not just the possible outcome. For example, ask:

  • What facts or records would you need to review before assessing my situation?
  • What steps might come next if the firm accepts the case?
  • How would you handle contact and negotiations with an insurance company?
  • In what circumstances might litigation be considered?
  • How will I receive updates, and whom should I contact with questions?
  • What does the contingency fee arrangement mean for me?

A lawyer shouldn't promise a particular result based on a brief description. Listen for a straightforward explanation of the review process and an honest account of what remains uncertain. Take notes so you can compare answers and decide at your own pace.

## Get your records in order
You don't need to build a complete file before asking for help, but a few items can make the conversation more specific. Gather any incident report or property documentation you have, photographs, contact details for witnesses, and messages connected to the event. Write down the dates of medical visits and the ways the injury has affected work or daily responsibilities. Include insurer letters and notes about phone conversations, if available.

Keep the notes factual. Separate what you saw from what someone else told you, and flag anything you aren't certain about. If new information comes to mind later, you can share it with the attorney rather than trying to fill gaps from memory during the first call.

## Know when to ask for legal guidance
Consider speaking with a lawyer when you have questions about responsibility, insurance requests, or how to preserve useful records. It can also be helpful when communications feel difficult to manage while you're focused on treatment and home obligations.
