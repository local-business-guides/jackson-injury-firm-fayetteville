Questions to ask before hiring

Use an initial conversation to understand how the firm would approach your situation. You can ask:

  • Who will answer my questions and give me updates?
  • What information would help you evaluate my situation?
  • What work may be needed to gather evidence?
  • How do insurance negotiations and possible litigation fit into the process?
  • How does the fee arrangement work?

A short checklist for your first conversation

Before speaking with a lawyer, write down the date and location of the incident, what happened, and the names of anyone who witnessed it. Make a simple list of the care you've received and any conversations you've had with an insurer. You don't need to organize every detail perfectly. A clear timeline can help you explain the situation without relying on memory alone.

Gather any records already in your possession, such as incident paperwork, photos, medical documents or messages about the event. Keep the originals safe, and tell the attorney if some records are still missing. Don't delay a call just because you haven't collected every document. An attorney can discuss what may be useful and whether more evidence should be gathered.

Mistakes that can make things harder

Try not to guess when describing details you don't remember. Be direct about what you know and what you're unsure of. Keep copies of insurer messages and documents, and avoid signing paperwork you don't understand before getting advice about it. A quick settlement offer may not answer all your questions about the claim, so ask what the document means before deciding how to respond.

A lawyer should explain the available steps without promising a particular result. Case evaluation, evidence gathering, insurance negotiations and litigation when appropriate can all be part of representation, depending on the circumstances.
